# Campaign alpha decisions

These extend [../vertical-slice/DECISIONS.md](../vertical-slice/DECISIONS.md) (VS-01 to VS-32 stay in force) for
`Rule_and_Ruin_Campaign_Alpha_Prompts_13_to_20.md`. Every value here is a **provisional design specification** from that pack or
chosen to implement it: reversible, documented, and not lore canon. Status values as in the vertical-slice register.

| ID | Decision | Why | Reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| CA-01 | **"The Passage War"** (working title) is a scenario configuration of the existing passage campaign (`PassageWar.Create`), not a new game or galaxy: the same 44 systems, factions, rules and saves. Its active scope is 10 systems (Forgefall, Ironmouth, Cinderhold, Gloam, Umbra, Salvage Row, Blackwake, Shoal Haven, Dividend, Ledgerhold); route planning stays inside them. Designated worlds: Forgefall and Ironmouth (Pygmaei), Dividend and Ledgerhold (Inhumanus). The freeform passage campaign (VS-19) and the Aurelith campaign are unchanged and still in the F6 menu. | Prompt 14: bounded scope without deleting the wider galaxy. Two routes join the bases: the passage at Umbra and the northern route through the Pirate Reach. | Remove the menu entries; `Slice.Scenario` stays null. |
| CA-02 | **Strategic time** keeps the existing model: a pausable real-time clock that advances whole days (1, 2.5 or 5 days a second). While an encounter or an operation is pending, time stops for both powers: no income, production, movement or settlement. Pausing or opening windows never pays income (income is paid only by `AdvanceDay`). Settlements fall on every 10th day and are keyed by day, so a reload can neither repeat nor skip one. | Prompt 14: no conversion of the time model; UI pauses grant nothing. | Change `ScenarioState.Interval`. |
| CA-03 | **Ending conditions.** Victory by arms: hold access won in battle at 3 consecutive settlements (the pack's provisional default) with both designated worlds operational and supply reaching Umbra; the count restarts while access is lost, a designated world is lost or blockaded, supply does not reach Umbra, an enemy flagship-led fleet holds Umbra, or the rival actively contests it (a declared objective and a fleet that can fight). Victory by treaty: passage rights by settlement or treaty, still in force at the next settlement. Either ends at the first counting settlement once the rival has no way left to contest (no fleet that can fight, none building or affordable, no attempts left, or a truce): no passive waiting after the outcome is certain. Defeat: three failed objectives with the rival holding access at a settlement, or no flagship-led fleet, none building and none affordable at a settlement (with a warning first). Inconclusive: day 240. A lost battle alone never ends the campaign. | Prompt 14: transparent start, victory, defeat and inconclusive end; no surprise conditions; every condition is in the mandate window. | Values in `ScenarioState`. |
| CA-04 | **Strategic treaty** (`PassageWar.ProposeTreaty`): between encounters, one proposal per side per settlement interval, with an optional payment of up to 100 credits. The other side decides by the existing negotiation rule (pressure against cost, every factor listed) from public facts: payment ÷ 4; +40 if it has no flagship-led fleet; +30 if one of its designated worlds is lost or blockaded; up to +30 for a weaker fleet (total hull); standing ÷ 2. Cost: 60 if it holds the passage, 30 if contested, 20 to recognise access the proposer won; +20 while it has never lost a battle over the passage. Accepted rights bring a truce and stop its fleets heading for the passage. | The pre-battle settlement (VS-22) carries no battle pressure, so it could never succeed: there was no negotiated route that avoided fighting. This extends the same verdict to the strategic layer. A blockade by the northern route makes a treaty possible without any battle. | Constants in `PassageWar.Treaty.cs`. |
| CA-05 | **Access basis and truces.** Passage access records how it was obtained (`battle` or `treaty`). Fleets of powers under a truce no longer start an engagement when they meet (`StrategyRules.OnArrival`, lane interception). | A settlement or treaty that still produced a battle on contact was not a truce. | `PassageWar.AtPeace`. |
| CA-06 | **Ledger and ending.** Each decision and its cost is written once by the transaction that made it (declarations, battles with losses, refits, repairs, production, salvage, treaties, settlements, withdrawals, upkeep). The ending recounts them, with battles won and lost, flagships lost, standing and who holds the passage. After the ending the galaxy is frozen until the player chooses "Continue freeform" (the outcome stays recorded). | Prompt 14: an ending that recounts actual decisions and costs. | `PassageWar.Ending`. |
| CA-07 | **Upkeep and arrears** (Passage War only): at each settlement both powers pay 2 credits per fleet battle point. What cannot be paid becomes arrears; a balance never goes below zero. While in arrears, projects pause and repairs, refits, commissions and new projects are refused with the reason; income pays arrears first. The AI is charged the same way and cannot commission a flagship in arrears. | Prompt 15: insolvency explained, no buying on credit, the same accounting for the AI. Starting yields are generous (+8 energy, +11 materials, +6 credits a day for the Pygmaei), so upkeep is what makes credits bind. | `Economy.UpkeepPerPoint`. |
| CA-08 | **Supply** (`Supply.Of`) is derived each time from public map facts, never stored: it starts at a polity's unblockaded shipyard systems (and a completed forward dock) and runs along lanes inside the scenario through its own systems, unclaimed systems and neutral ports. It stops at a system where a hostile fleet holds position, an orbit a hostile power holds, a passage whose access the polity lacks (a contested passage carries nobody's supply), and rival territory (unless at truce). Known threats are hostile fleets whose visible orders pass through a supplied system, with the day they arrive. The strategic map has no fog of war in this project, so nothing hidden can leak; if fog is added, `Passable` must read only known positions. Transport capacity is not modelled for supply. | Prompt 15: a legible overlay from real routes, access and blockades. | `Supply.cs`. |
| CA-09 | **Supply effects** are visible and strategic only: whether the hold at Umbra counts (CA-03), whether a forward dock is restocked, and whether convoy offices earn. Cuts and threats are announced once each in the dispatch, and effects apply at the next settlement. No combat value changes with supply. | Prompt 15: a predictable decision window; no hidden damage modifiers or tactical effects. | Remove the checks. |
| CA-10 | **Local priorities** use the existing project system (a new `ProjectKind.Priority`; one project per world): Pygmaei forward repair dock (Cinderhold; 40 materials, 40 credits, 12 days), Ironmouth ore drive (+4 materials a day unless blockaded), convoy office (Forgefall; +3 credits a day while supply reaches Shoal Haven; it occupies the shipyard for 10 days); Inhumanus exchange convoys (Dividend; +4 credits), Ledgerhold ore contract (+4 materials), escort underwriting (Ledgerhold; escorts commissioned at Dividend cost 25% fewer credits). Each shows its effect, what it enables and its opportunity cost before it is started. | Prompt 15: distinct practical functions for worlds and a bounded choice of priorities, without a new currency. | `Economy.Priorities`. |
| CA-11 | **Forward dock stores**: a finite stock of materials (at most 60) held at the dock, restocked with 30 from the national stock at each settlement only while the dock is supplied. Repairs at the dock draw materials from the stores (energy and credits from the national stock); exhausted stores refuse a repair with the amounts. | Prompt 15: finite local stores and an exhausted-stores case. | `Economy.DockCapacity`, `DockRestock`. |
| CA-12 | **One war-operation state machine** (`Operations`): objective and stakes, committed forces, orbital supply defence, the defender's choice, the attacker's choice (land, terms or withdraw), the planetary series, the final settlement. Orbital engagements and planetary matches are ordinary Commandfall battles through `BattleSession`; nothing else resolves combat. A battle applies only its own casualties (keyed by battle id); only the operation's final legal outcome (the attacker winning the series, or the defender yielding under terms) changes who owns a world. Strategic time stops for both powers while an operation is in progress, and saves between engagements and during a battle are supported. | Prompt 16: battle, operation and campaign victory stay distinct. | `Operations*.cs` |
| CA-13 | **Orbital supply defence**, as the pack states it: the defender needs one victory in up to two engagements to protect the world's supplies. Won first: it may stop, or elect a follow-up. Lost first: a forced second chance. Attacker 2-0: it seizes 40 materials of supplies and the garrison's reserves are cut off. **Provisional follow-up stakes** (recorded for review): the first defensive victory locks in protection; the elected follow-up risks the defender's committed ships for 30 materials of the attacker's supplies and prisoners by boarding (the capture policy is on for that engagement); losing it never revokes the protection. No random capture roll. With no defending fleet, the defence is uncontested: supplies are not protected and nothing is seized. | Pack Prompt 16. | `Operations.SupplyMaterials`, `FollowUpMaterials` |
| CA-14 | **Planetary series**: best of three; first to two wins; a 2-0 stops without a third match; destroying a commander ends that match only. Each match deploys at most 6 units within the 30-point Full Roster budget, the commander first, then the strongest (for the Inhumanus, the Corporate Charter that most of the force belongs to, its specialists first). Maps rotate through the three planetary boards from a start fixed by the operation's id. Destroyed units stay destroyed and damage carries over; positions, statuses, heat, cooldowns and objectives reset every match. Undeployed units are reserves: the attacker's always; the defender's only while its supplies are protected (otherwise only the units of its first deployment may fight again). | Pack Prompt 16 (near the five-minute ground battle as a test hypothesis; unmeasured with people). | `Operations.DeployCap`, `Lineup` |
| CA-15 | **Draws and exhausted rounds**: Commandfall matches never draw. At the round limit the existing rule decides on objectives held, then surviving army value, then the commander's hull, and a full tie goes to the defender, so a series always ends by its third match. | Pack: an explicit policy for draws and exhausted rounds. | Existing rule (`RosterRules.ResolveRoundLimit`). |
| CA-16 | **Command succession (provisional)**: when a side's flagship or commander has been lost, it names an acting commander from its surviving committed units before the next engagement (the AI and the default pick the most valuable, then the lowest uid; the player may choose). The acting commander carries a `command:acting` token: it is the army's one leader and its loss ends the battle. Nobody is resurrected. A side that cannot deploy a commander concedes that engagement or match (recorded, no battle fought). Rules patch **vs-4** ([VENDOR_PATCHES](../vertical-slice/VENDOR_PATCHES.md)); battles recorded under vs-3 are refused. | Commandfall armies need exactly one leader, and each faction has one leader class per domain, so no lawful successor existed. | Remove the token. |
| CA-17 | **Ground forces and transport**: ground units are real roster units held as garrisons on worlds or embarked in fleets, never invisible. Starting forces: a legion at each home world and a garrison on each second designated world (8 units each; tier III non-leaders under the Balanced unlock). Transport (provisional): a flagship carries two units, every other ship one; units beyond a fleet's remaining capacity after a loss are lost with their transports and recorded. | Pack: an invasion manifest tied to actual forces and capacity. | `Operations.Capacity` |
| CA-18 | **Terms in an operation**: the attacker may offer that the defender yield the world, its garrison leaving intact for another of its worlds. The defender decides by pressure against cost (from what both sides can see): +40 if the world's supplies were seized, +30 if the series stands against it, up to +30 for weaker ground forces; cost 60. Withdrawal is always legal between engagements: the fleet and its troops leave, the orbit returns to the owner, nothing changes hands. | Pack: optional withdrawal or negotiation; an accepted settlement as a legal final outcome. | Constants in `Operations`. |
| CA-19 | **Art for ground units**: the Pygmaei and Inhumanus planetary classes have no models; the battle view shows the existing placeholder. Recorded as an asset gap. | No approved art exists; the pack does not authorise new production. | Add models to the art library. |
| CA-20 | **The strategic rival** (`RivalAI`): each day, one economic action and one movement plan per AI power, chosen from a fixed list of candidate plans scored by integer formulas with a stable tie-break (hold, defend the passage it holds, contest the passage, raid an unguarded system on the enemy's supply line when the enemy fleet is at least 20 days from it, relieve a blockaded or lost designated world, go home and repair; commission a replacement flagship or an escort, a local priority, a doctrine, a treaty purchase, a status-quo truce). A committed movement plan is kept unless another scores 30 more (no oscillation). Doctrine priorities change thresholds and weights: Pygmaei attack at 85% of the enemy's hull, +20 aggression, +15 industry, no treaties; Inhumanus at 120%, +20 commerce, +25 treaty will. Reasons go to a developer log in the save (`AiLog`), never to the player's dispatch. In the Passage War it replaces the slice's scheduled counter-offensive (VS-28); the disclosed answer to a declaration (its fleet sails in two days) is kept. | Prompt 17. | `RivalAI.cs` |
| CA-21 | **Information limits**: the rival plans from a `Belief` of public facts: fleet positions, classes and hulls on the map, the lane a moving fleet is visibly on (never its destination), orbits, access, relations, history. It never reads the other side's orders beyond that lane, treasury, projects, fitted modules or dock stores. Scenario-authored public information is not cheating: the strategic map shows every fleet in this project, and the scenario discloses the rival's answer to a declaration. AI moves the player is told about now name only the next system, matching what the AI may know of the player's. | Prompt 17: the difference between authored public information and cheating. | `Belief.Of` |
| CA-22 | **Proposals to the player and broken truces**: an AI proposal (passage rights for credits, or a status-quo truce) waits for the player's answer until the next settlement; accepting applies the same transaction an AI acceptance would. A status-quo truce ends the campaign as an inconclusive, negotiated end. Declaring war over a truce is a recorded violation costing 30 standing. One proposal per side per interval (no negotiation spam). | Prompt 17: enforceable settlements, honoured and violated treaties, a lawful way to end a war. | `PassageWar.AnswerProposal`, `ProposeStatusQuo` |
| CA-23 | **Doctrine** (Prompt 18): six projects per faction on two paths, paid in the existing resources (influence included), one at a time, paused in arrears; the first project commits the path (the other path costs double and its last project is foreclosed); completed projects are never refunded and a cancelled one returns half. Effects reuse what exists: lane travel inside the supply network, the forward dock, foundry yields, build times, tolls and treaty payments, convoy income, repair costs, operation reserves, and four tactical sidegrades carried as disclosed army-entry tokens (rules patch **vs-5**: per-ship overclock and contract values). No satisfaction or legitimacy system exists, so policy effects are economic and broader politics are deferred. Faction lines are provisional writing pending approval. | Prompt 18. | `Doctrines.cs` |
| CA-24 | **Replay variants** (Prompt 19): four hand-authored starts on the same campaign: the standard Passage War, the open corridor (fleets a jump forward, toll 5, the derelict charted), the narrow network (no northern route, a third fewer credits) and the salvage field (wrecked passage, three salvageable wrecks, damaged flagships, scarce materials, content loadouts). Stored with the setup seed in the save; each passes route, deployment, supply, succession, recovery, objective and topology checks (`Variants.Validate`). | Prompt 19: variation through situations, not dice or inflated statistics. | `Variants.cs` |
| CA-25 | **The content pipeline** (Prompt 19): capital-ship modules and scenario loadouts are authored in `Assets/RR/Content/slice_content.json`, validated and generated into `SliceContent.g.cs` by `Tools/Content/rr_content.py` (unknown fields are refused: new behaviour needs a rules handler, its preview, AI legality and a test first), and checked again at start-up against the live roster. The content fingerprint includes the modules (rules patch **vs-6**), so a battle saved under other content is refused. Exercised with a new sidegrade on existing hulls, **extended magazines** (+1 range, −1 damage), with no rules code. | Prompt 19. | `rr_content.py` |
| CA-26 | **The ship manifest** (Prompt 19): a read-only reference generated from the live registry (`SliceTests manifest`): id, faction, class, role, tier, points, hull, armour, movement, attack, leadership and protection rules, legal configuration, silhouette asset, and the rules stamp; dimensions are not modelled for play (one hex per ship). Provisional values; not a miniature specification. | Prompt 19. | `ShipManifest.cs` |
| CA-27 | **Occupation** (Prompt 20): at a settlement, a power at war with a declared objective at the passage whose flagship-led fleet holds position at Umbra, with no hostile fleet able to fight there, takes access by arms (basis battle); its hold counts from the next settlement. The same rule applies to the rival. | The Pygmaei journey found a blocker: with the defender's fleets gone, access could only change hands through a battle that could no longer happen, so a won war ended inconclusive. | `PassageWar.Occupation`; `PassageWarTests.Occupation_AnUnopposedFleetAtThePassage...`. |
| CA-28 | **Battle analysis off the frame** (Prompt 20): the Command Threat analysis (threats to both flagships and the round exposure) runs on a worker thread on a copy of the state and is published only if the state has not changed; the flagship warning is raised when it is published. | Measured at about 2 s at a battle's opening position (3,191 orders checked), which froze the first battle frame for about 3.5 s. | `BattlePresentation.RefreshAnalysis`. |
| CA-29 | **Faction identity** (the creator, 2026-10-20): the Pygmaei use the creator's new emblem (`Resources/RR/Emblems/pygmaei.png`); the Inhumanus are gold (map colour `#F2C044`, gold nebula, gold ship trim, emblem and ship portraits recoloured) and the Pirates green (`#4FD86A`, green nebula, emblem recoloured). The palette lives in `SliceScenario.Palette` and is applied again when a save loads, so existing saves take the new colours. The Pygmaei map colour stays violet. | The creator's direction; the two slice factions no longer share red. | `SliceScenario.Palette`; the old emblems are in git history. |
