# Campaign alpha rules summary

Provisional design values throughout (codex 0.1 draft, slice and campaign-alpha experiments). Full decisions:
`Docs/vertical-slice/DECISIONS.md` (VS-01 to VS-32) and `Docs/campaign-alpha/DECISIONS.md` (CA-01 onward).

**Battles.** Generals don't roll dice: no random hits, damage or kills. Every attack shows its result before you commit it. In
a leader-elimination battle a legal lethal attack on the enemy flagship or commander ends the battle at once. Numbers protect
nobody; only rules and positioning do (blocked lines, armour and shields, range, screening, declared interception). Capital
ships cannot fire their main battery into the 120° astern. Pygmaei ships may overclock (heat; overheated at 4). Inhumanus
escorts may be contracted to arrive in round 3 on a disclosed hex. At the round limit, objectives, then strength, then the
commander's hull decide; a full tie goes to the defender.

**The Passage War.** Secure durable access through the Umbra passage and keep your two designated worlds operational.
Settlements every 10 days. Victory by arms: hold access won in battle through 3 consecutive settlements while supplied and
uncontested. Access by arms also comes from occupation: at war over the passage, a fleet led by a flagship that holds Umbra at a
settlement with no enemy fleet able to fight there takes access (either side); the hold counts from the next settlement. Victory by treaty: passage rights by treaty, ratified at the next settlement. Defeat: three failed objectives with
the rival holding access, or no flagship-led fleet and no way to build one. Inconclusive: day 240, or a status-quo truce.
A lost battle alone never ends the campaign. While an encounter or an operation is pending, time stops for both powers.

**Economy and supply.** Upkeep (2 credits per fleet point) at each settlement; unpaid upkeep becomes arrears (projects pause,
no new spending) and never a negative balance. Local priorities (forward dock, ore drive, convoy office; exchange convoys,
ore contract, escort underwriting) are projects with stated effects and opportunity costs. Supply runs from shipyards (and a
forward dock) through your systems, unclaimed systems and neutral ports; enemy fleets holding a system, hostile orbits and
passage access you do not hold stop it. Supply decides whether your hold counts, docks restock and convoys earn.

**Treaties.** One proposal per side per settlement interval. The other side weighs pressure (payment, a missing flagship, a
blockaded or lost world, a weaker fleet, standing) against cost (the passage, and more while it has never lost a battle over
it); every factor is shown. A truce stops contact battles; breaking one costs standing.

**Operations.** Invading a rival world: up to two orbital engagements decide its supplies (the defender needs one win; it may
stop, or elect a follow-up that never revokes what it earned), then a best-of-three planetary series (first to two; 2-0 stops).
Only the series or the defender yielding under terms transfers the world. Lost commanders are replaced by an acting commander
from surviving units; a side that cannot deploy one concedes. Troops travel aboard ships (flagship two, others one).

**Doctrine.** Two paths per faction; the first project commits you (the other path costs double and its last project is
foreclosed). Tactical sidegrades are disclosed to both sides before deployment.

**The rival.** Plans from what its side can see (fleets on the map, orbits, access, history; never your orders beyond a lane,
your treasury, projects, modules or stores), through the same commands and costs as you.
